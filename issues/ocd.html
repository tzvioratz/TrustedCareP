OCD

{% include accordion.html number='1' title='Body Dysmorphic Disorder (BDD)' main='Body Dysmorphic Disorder involves obsessive concern about perceived flaws in appearance—often things others barely notice or don’t see at all. Individuals may spend large amounts of time checking mirrors, comparing themselves to others, seeking reassurance, or trying to “fix” the concern. BDD can greatly affect self-esteem, relationships, and daily functioning, but effective treatment can help people build confidence and reduce obsessive focus on appearance.' %} {% include accordion.html number='2' title='Checking OCD' main='Checking OCD is characterized by repeated checking behaviors driven by fear that something bad will happen if things aren’t verified repeatedly. This may include checking locks, appliances, emails, doors, or tasks “just in case.” Even when someone knows they already checked, the doubt feels overwhelming. Treatment helps reduce anxiety and regain trust in one’s memory and safety.' %} {% include accordion.html number='3' title='Contamination OCD' main='Contamination OCD involves intense fear of germs, illness, dirt, or “being contaminated,” leading to compulsions like excessive cleaning, washing, or avoidance of places or people. These fears feel very real and distressing, often taking over daily life. With the right help, people can reduce fear and regain comfort in everyday environments.' %} {% include accordion.html number='4' title='Existential OCD' main='Existential OCD centers on obsessive questioning about life, purpose, reality, or meaning in ways that cause distress rather than curiosity. People may feel stuck in endless mental spirals, unable to feel present or calm. Treatment helps individuals step out of these cycles and reconnect with life in a grounded way.' %} {% include accordion.html number='5' title='False Memory OCD' main='False Memory OCD involves intrusive doubts or memories about events that may or may not have happened, often involving guilt, fear of consequences, or worry about being a “bad person.” Even without evidence, the uncertainty feels unbearable. Treatment focuses on reducing mental checking and restoring trust in one’s integrity.' %} {% include accordion.html number='6' title='Harm OCD' main='Harm OCD includes intrusive, unwanted thoughts about hurting oneself or others, despite having no desire to cause harm. These thoughts are disturbing, frightening, and deeply distressing. Treatment helps individuals understand these thoughts as anxiety-driven and regain a sense of safety and self-trust.' %} {% include accordion.html number='7' title='“Just Right” OCD' main='“Just Right” OCD focuses on the need for things to feel “perfect,” balanced, or exactly right. A person might repeat actions, adjust objects, or redo tasks until they get a feeling of completion or relief. Treatment helps reduce reliance on these rituals and build tolerance for normal imperfection.' %} {% include accordion.html number='8' title='Magical Thinking OCD' main='Magical Thinking OCD involves the belief that thoughts, actions, or rituals can prevent bad things from happening, even if logically unrelated. People may feel responsible for preventing harm through specific mental or physical rituals. Treatment helps break the link between thoughts and perceived responsibility.' %} {% include accordion.html number='9' title='Pedophilia OCD (POCD)' main='Pedophilia OCD involves intrusive, unwanted thoughts or fears about being sexually inappropriate toward children, despite these thoughts being deeply distressing and completely against the person’s values. This is anxiety-driven, not desire-driven, and often causes intense shame and avoidance. With compassionate treatment, individuals can learn to manage these thoughts and feel safe and grounded again.' %} {% include accordion.html number='10' title='Perinatal OCD' main='Perinatal OCD occurs during pregnancy or after childbirth and includes intrusive fears about harm coming to the baby, often accompanied by compulsions like checking, avoidance, or reassurance-seeking. These thoughts are unwanted and distressing, not reflective of intentions. Treatment provides relief, reassurance, and confidence in parenting.' %} {% include accordion.html number='11' title='Racist OCD' main='Racist OCD involves intrusive thoughts, images, or fears about being racist or causing harm through offensive thoughts or behaviors, even though the person values equality and respect. The anxiety leads to compulsive mental checking, reassurance seeking, or avoidance. Treatment helps individuals separate intrusive thoughts from identity.' %} {% include accordion.html number='12' title='Real Event OCD' main='Real Event OCD centers on guilt or shame about something that truly happened in the past, even if it was minor or resolved. The person becomes stuck in mental replay, self-doubt, and constant moral questioning. Treatment helps reduce rumination and restore self-compassion.' %} {% include accordion.html number='13' title='Relationship OCD (ROCD)' main='Relationship OCD involves obsessive doubt about one’s relationship, partner, or feelings—worrying whether the relationship is right, whether they truly love their partner, or whether their partner truly loves them. This can lead to checking behaviors, avoidance, or endless reassurance seeking. Treatment helps individuals find clarity, trust, and emotional stability.' %} {% include accordion.html number='14' title='Religious OCD / Scrupulosity' main='Scrupulosity involves intense fear of being spiritually wrong, sinful, or morally flawed, leading to compulsive prayer, confession, checking, or avoidance of religious situations. These fears feel deeply distressing and can interfere with religious life rather than support it. Treatment helps reduce anxiety while respecting personal faith and values.' %} {% include accordion.html number='15' title='Sensorimotor OCD' main='Sensorimotor OCD involves becoming overly aware of automatic bodily processes such as breathing, blinking, swallowing, or heartbeat. This awareness feels “stuck,” making it hard to focus on anything else. Treatment helps retrain the brain to let sensations return to the background naturally.' %} {% include accordion.html number='16' title='Sexual OCD' main='Sexual OCD involves intrusive, unwanted sexual thoughts that are distressing, inappropriate, or completely out of character. These thoughts create shame, anxiety, and fear about identity or morality. Treatment focuses on reducing fear, guilt, and compulsive mental checking, helping individuals reclaim comfort and peace of mind.' %} {% include accordion.html number='17' title='Sexual Orientation OCD (SO-OCD)' main='Sexual Orientation OCD involves intrusive doubts or fears about one’s sexual orientation, even when there has been no prior confusion. People may constantly analyze feelings, test reactions, or seek reassurance. Treatment helps break the cycle of doubt and restore confidence in identity.' %} {% include accordion.html number='18' title='Staring OCD' main='Staring OCD involves intrusive concerns about staring at people inappropriately, making others uncomfortable, or doing something socially unacceptable with one’s gaze. This often leads to hyper-awareness of eye contact and avoidance. Treatment helps reduce fear and restore natural, comfortable social interactions.' %} {% include accordion.html number='19' title='Suicidal OCD' main='Suicidal OCD includes intrusive, unwanted thoughts about suicide that are fear-based and horrifying to the person experiencing them, not reflective of a desire to die. These thoughts cause panic, avoidance, and reassurance seeking. With treatment, individuals can learn to manage these thoughts and feel safe again. (If someone is actually feeling unsafe or wants to harm themselves, that’s a separate, urgent clinical situation.)' %} {% include accordion.html number='20' title='Symmetry and Ordering OCD' main='Symmetry and Ordering OCD involves an intense need for objects, movements, or visual spaces to feel balanced, aligned, or perfectly organized. A person may spend large amounts of time arranging, counting, or repeating behaviors until things feel “right.” Treatment helps reduce compulsions and restore flexibility and comfort.' %} {% include accordion.html number='21' title='Tourettic OCD' main='Tourettic OCD involves a blend of OCD-like compulsions and tic-like behaviors, often involving repeated actions or phrases driven by a strong internal urge or sensation rather than fear-based obsession alone. Treatment is tailored to both the OCD and tic components to provide meaningful relief.' %}
